You will compute compression ratios, trace tensor shapes from waveform to discrete IDs, work a vector-quantization example by hand, and connect everything back to text tokenization from your foundations unit.

Bridge from Lesson 2 (MIDI): MIDI stores events — “note 60, velocity 80, 0.25 s.” That is a human-designed token alphabet (~128 pitches × timing). It excels at editing structure but cannot encode breath noise, guitar distortion, or room reverb.

Bridge from foundations (text tokens): GPT-style models consume integer IDs from a BPE vocabulary. Training optimizes P(wt | w<t). Audio needs an analogous map from waveform → IDs so the same transformer machinery applies to P(at | a<t).

This lesson’s answer: Learn a neural codec whose codebooks are the “audio vocabulary.”

Mini-glossary (Lesson 3)

  • Codec — encoder/decoder pair that compresses audio to a compact representation.
  • Codebook — matrix of V prototype vectors; each row is a “sound atom.”
  • Frame rate — how many latent summaries per second (MusicGen: ~50 Hz).
  • RVQ — residual vector quantization; stack codebooks for coarse→fine.
  • Interleaving — order for predicting multiple codebooks across time.

1. The scale problem: why not predict samples?

Autoregressive models predict one discrete token per step. Treating each PCM sample as a token seems natural — but the sequence length explodes.

Worked example A — 10 s mono @ 16 kHz
  • Sample rate fs = 16,000 → 16,000 numbers per second.
  • Duration 10 s → T = 10 × 16,000 = 160,000 samples.
  • One AR step per sample → 160k forward passes; causal attention cost grows ~O(T²) in naive form.
  • Storage (16-bit PCM): 160,000 × 16 bits = 2.56 Mbit per channel.
Worked example B — same clip @ 44.1 kHz (CD quality)
  • T = 10 × 44,100 = 441,000 steps — impractical for GPT-scale attention at full length.
  • This is why Lesson 1 spectrograms help human analysis but do not alone fix AR length — pixel-level spectrogram AR is also huge unless downsampled.
Target: codec frames @ 50 Hz, K = 4 codebooks
  • Frames in 10 s: F = 10 × 50 = 500.
  • Discrete decisions ≈ 500 × 4 = 2,000 (vs 160,000–441,000 samples).
  • Sequence shortening factor vs 16 kHz samples: 160,000 / 2,000 = 80×.
Quiz 1 (numerical): 30 s stereo @ 48 kHz — samples per channel?
Show answer

30 × 48,000 = 1,440,000 per channel.

Analogy — BPE on a tiny string (from foundations)

Corpus merges teach that "ing" is a reusable piece. Sentence "coding" might tokenize to [code, ing] → IDs [4012, 988].

Audio analogue: a frame’s latent might quantize to RVQ IDs [402, 88, 1201, 55] — four entries whose sum reconstructs the frame latent, like four subword vectors summing to meaning.

MIDI vs codec (Lesson 2 tie-in): MIDI token NOTE_ON 60 says which key, not how the grand piano in this hall sounds. Codec tokens capture the recording-grade timbre MIDI omits.

3. Autoencoder: encode → bottleneck → decode

The codec learns a compressed representation by reconstruction:

  1. Encoder E: waveform window → continuous latent z.
  2. Quantizer Q: z → nearest codebook IDs (information bottleneck).
  3. Decoder D: quantized latents → reconstructed waveform x̂.
Loss (conceptual):   ‖ x − D(Q(E(x))) ‖²  + λadv · discriminator(x, x̂)  + λpercept · perceptual_feat(x, x̂)

Adversarial and perceptual terms push reconstructions to sound good, not merely minimize MSE on samples (which favors blurry audio).

4. Vector quantization — worked 2-D example

For each frame latent z ∈ ℝD, VQ picks argmink ‖z − ek‖².

Given z = [0.2, 0.9], codebook (V=4):
IDek‖z − ek‖²
0[0, 0]0.04 + 0.81 = 0.85
1[1, 0]0.64 + 0.81 = 1.45
2[0, 1]0.04 + 0.01 = 0.05 ✓
3[1, 1]0.64 + 0.01 = 0.65

Output ID = 2, reconstruction [0, 1], error vector [0.2, −0.1].

Quiz 2: z = [0.6, 0.1] with same codebook — winner?
Show answer

ID 1 ([1,0]) with distance 0.17.

5. Residual VQ (RVQ) — coarse then fine

  1. Quantize z with book 1 → ê(1), residual r(1) = z − ê(1).
  2. Quantize r(1) with book 2 → add to reconstruction.
  3. Repeat for K books (MusicGen commonly K=4).
MusicGen / EnCodec public specs (typical checkpoint)
  • Output audio: mono 32 kHz
  • Frame rate: 50 Hz (20 ms per frame)
  • Codebooks: K = 4
  • Entries per book: V ≈ 2048
  • Token throughput: 50 × 4 = 200 tokens/s
Compression ratio vs raw 32 kHz 16-bit mono
  • Raw bitrate: 32,000 × 16 = 512 kbps
  • Idealized token bits: 200 × log₂(2048) = 200 × 11 ≈ 2.2 kbps
  • Ratio ≈ 512 / 2.2 ≈ 230× — explains feasibility of AR modeling.
Quiz 3: 8 s clip — frames F at 50 Hz? Total IDs with K=4?
Show answer

F = 400; IDs ≤ 400 × 4 = 1600.

6. Shape trail (10 s @ 32 kHz)

x waveform: (T,) T = 10 × 32,000 = 320,000 E(x) latents: (F, D) F = 500, D ≈ 128 (typical order-of-magnitude) Q latents: (F, K) K = 4 integer IDs ∈ [0, 2047] Lookup + sum: (F, D) embed IDs → vectors, sum per frame D(·) waveform: (T,) 320,000 samples out

The LM predicts the flattened/interleaved stream derived from (F, K), not the 320k floats directly.

7. Interleaving & codebook delay

Predicting all K books at frame t independently wastes coarse→fine structure. MusicGen applies a delay pattern so coarse books lead fine books across time steps.

Why not independent codebooks? Fine detail (book 4) should condition on coarse outline (book 1) already chosen — mirroring RVQ’s residual design and reducing impossible ID combinations.
Quiz 4 (bits): F=500, V=1024, K=4 — approximate total bits?
Show answer

500 × 4 × log₂(1024) = 20,000 bits. General: bits ≈ F · K · log₂(V).

Quiz 5: Decoder’s role after MusicGen finishes predicting tokens?
Show answer

Map codec IDs → latents → upsample to PCM waveform for playback.

8. Step-by-step generation mechanics (codec + LM)

  1. Initialize empty token list (or copy prefix IDs for continuation — Lesson 4).
  2. For each AR step t: transformer outputs logits vector length V over next codebook entry.
  3. Sample ID (greedy, temperature, top-p — same ideas as text GPT).
  4. Interleaver maps step index → (frame index, codebook index) via delay schedule.
  5. After all frames: ID lookup → latent grid (F, D).
  6. Decoder transposed convolutions / upsampling → (T,) waveform.
# Simplified single-step view (one interleaved position) logits = LM(audio_tokens[:t], text_condition) # shape (V,) next_id = categorical_sample(softmax(logits / temperature)) audio_tokens.append(next_id) # ... repeat ~2000 times for 10s ... waveform = CodecDecoder(audio_tokens)

11. Training vs inference (two-phase story)

Production systems almost always train the codec first, then train (or fine-tune) the language model on frozen codec tokens.

PhaseWhat learnsDataLoss intuition
Codec pretrainEncoder, decoder, codebooksRaw audio clipsReconstruct waveform + perceptual/adversarial
LM trainTransformer over discrete IDs(text, audio) pairs tokenizedNext-token cross-entropy
InferenceNothing (weights frozen)User promptSample tokens → decode once

You do not backpropagate from speaker air pressure into the codec during MusicGen inference — the decoder is a fixed “sound card” for token sequences.

12. Micro walkthrough — one 20 ms frame

Frame index f = 42 at 32 kHz, 50 Hz framing:
  • Covers samples roughly [33600, 34240) (640 samples ≈ 20 ms).
  • Encoder emits continuous z42 ∈ ℝ128.
  • RVQ selects IDs (k1, k2, k3, k4) e.g. (402, 88, 1201, 55).
  • Reconstruction ẑ42 = e(1)402 + e(2)88 + …
  • Decoder uses ẑ42 with neighbors to synthesize those 640 samples.

During generation, the LM never sees z directly — it predicts the four IDs using past IDs + (Lesson 4) text condition.

13. Common misconceptions (check yourself)

MythReality
“Spectrogram pixels are the tokens”MusicGen uses learned codec IDs, not raw STFT bins (other models may differ).
“Each token is one note”Tokens are ~20 ms acoustic atoms; one note may span many frames.
“Bigger V always better”Diminishing returns; training stability and data matter too.
“Decoder invents new sounds”Decoder only combines codebook entries it was trained on.
Quiz 6 (numerical): Raw 10 s @ 32 kHz vs 2000 codec steps — what is the AR sequence shortening factor vs sample-level?
Show answer

320,000 / 2,000 = 160× fewer autoregressive steps than sample-level (same clip length).

Quiz 7: If V=2048 and you store one ID per frame (K=1 only) for 10 s @ 50 Hz, approximate bits?
Show answer

500 × log₂(2048) = 500 × 11 = 5500 bits (~687 bytes). RVQ uses K=4 for quality, not because one book lacks bits.

Metaphor (reprise): A codebook is a paint palette with V premixed colors. RVQ lets you layer up to K colors per stroke. The LM is the painter choosing palette indices; the decoder is the printer that turns indices into audible paint on the canvas of time.
